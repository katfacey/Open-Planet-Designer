# Open Planet Designer 1.2.1 — living surface geometry

The **1.3 realism revision** is documented in `PLANET_REALISM_GUIDE.md`, with its separate review under `output/planets/realism/`. This document retains the 1.2.1 baseline's behavior and measurements for reference.

The living-surface mode adds physical terrain, camera-local grass/tree/rock instances and animated geometric ocean waves. It uses PBR materials, real scene lights and shadows. The original orbital library and the version 1.1 entry file are retained separately.

## Open and use

Open `output/planets/living/Open_Planet_Living_Biomes.blend` with the complete version 1.2 add-on enabled. The file opens in the Forest close view at frame 240. Select the terrain, open **N → Open Planet**, choose a **Shot camera**, then scrub **frames 1–240**. Grassland, Forest, Ocean, Alpine and Desert cameras visit different fixed locations on the same climate-driven world. The Scene camera drives LOD when the LOD-camera override is empty.

Expand **Living Surface · Real Geometry** to change biome, heights, density, scatter distance, triangle budget and instance cap. **Camera Detail & Entry** controls terrain triangles, target pixels and camera distance fades. Refresh Camera Detail applies changes immediately; automatic changes are deferred until Blender finishes its frame evaluation.

For an existing rocky planet, **Build / Upgrade Living Surface** rebuilds its material/geometry graphs while preserving its controller. Very small scenes are enlarged internally to a radius of 10,000, with corresponding camera positions, location keyframes and orthographic framing scaled to preserve the shot. This avoids tiny near-clipping and shadow limits. Retain a copy of an existing production scene before upgrading. The supplied living file uses an Earth-sized display interpretation: 637.1 metres per Blender unit.

## What is actual geometry

- The cube-sphere is genuinely displaced. Mountain and canyon shapes combine world-anchored procedural fields with normalized, resampled USGS elevation data for Mount Rainier and the Grand Canyon. The data is artistically remapped into fictional regions; this is not a geographic reconstruction.
- Grass combines curved blade ribbons and textured CC0 grass tufts. Three different pine models have trunk, branch and textured foliage geometry. Their imported source models retain **416,451 / 267,343 / 347,991** triangles at the closest generator LOD; each has a **24,000**-triangle middle LOD and approximately **1,200**-triangle far LOD. The imported pine meshes are the provider's LOD2 variants, rather than its largest LOD0 variants. Scanned boulders have **66,122 / 3,000 / 180** triangles. Grass-tuft LODs have **659 / 239 / 64**. Fern undergrowth has **784 / 700 / 99**. Foliage includes alpha-cutout leaf/needle surfaces, as supplied by the source models.
- Repeated models remain shared Geometry Nodes instances. They are not expanded into millions of duplicate mesh datablocks. The budget counts their expanded triangle cost, not just prototype storage.
- Placements use stable, seeded cube-face cells. Climate weights and height sampling come from the same geometry graph that builds the planet. A ray test attaches models to the actual evaluated LOD mesh. At distance, individual trees give way to a geometric canopy proxy.
- Instance IDs retain each seeded cell's identity. The repeat-visit check requires identical cell IDs and allows a `2e-9`-radius attachment tolerance (about 13 mm with the Earth interpretation); observed maximum ray-attachment drift was about 4.8 mm. Finite float precision still applies.
- Grass uses five overlapping distance bands of progressively wider, lower-detail clumps, rather than a single lattice ending about 30 m away. The outer clumps approximate coverage by widening their horizontal footprint; they do not preserve every individual blade. Dense foreground coverage can consume the instance cap before the requested distance is reached. The scatter distance is an upper bound: size-based sampling limits and the triangle/instance caps can reduce actual reach. Forest patches vary in density, tree height and crown width, and reserve part of the scatter budget for undergrowth.
- Distant vegetated terrain transitions to canopy colour, then resolves to the photographed floor and actual plant instances nearby. This prevents a far forest from looking like exposed pale soil.
- Lowland ground includes continuous relief between continental scales and fine soil grain. The close geometric soil displacement is centimetre scale; photographed texture detail is supplementary.
- Eight moving wave components displace water vertices radially and tangentially. Fine water ripples and photographed ground microdetail supplement the geometry in the material. The scalar-driver path is rescaled before converting to radius units so very small controls do not arrive at the graph as zero.

## Performance controls

The terrain budget and scatter budget are separate hard caps. A default new living surface requests 60,000 terrain triangles and 600,000 expanded scatter triangles. The supplied review scene requests higher quality: **500,000 terrain / 3,000,000 scatter**, plus a 16,000-instance cap. Lower these for interactive navigation. Budget exhaustion selects lower model LODs or omits additional placements.

The living panel displays sizes and distances in scene length units (metres or kilometres in the supplied file). Internally, the custom properties remain fractions of radius, so resizing a planet scales its ecology consistently. The separate Camera Detail panel still uses radius fractions. With the supplied Earth-sized interpretation, the default tree height is about 22 m, grass height about 0.7 m and boulder size about 1.9 m. The geometry is finite, and maximum terrain refinement is 24. Texture maps and topology also have finite resolution.

The generator keeps the active surface cell near a **Floating Origin** rig and stores nearby terrain and placements relative to that cell. Planet and entry cameras share that rig. Parent foreground props to the same rig when they must move with the logical planetary scene. This is a camera-oriented precision system, not a complete multi-planet physics engine. Independently animated cameras added later need suitable parenting and a checked path.

CPU topology/scatter rebuilding is an offline-shot workflow at the high review settings; it is not a 60-fps terrain engine. Lower-detail hardware has not been benchmarked. A triangle cap is not a total GPU-memory or render-time cap. Alpha foliage, reflections, lighting, samples and other objects still cost rendering time.

The **Shadow distance** setting bounds the terrain patch that casts native sun shadows. Far terrain remains visible, while its enormous globe-wide bounds are excluded from that light's blocker collection. This avoids Eevee shadow-precision failures. The default is 0.004 radii (about 25 km for Earth); shadow casting is truncated beyond this camera region. Shadow texels also become coarser with camera altitude, preventing the shadow page pool from overflowing during an approach. Local vegetation and rocks still cast real shadows. Add custom foreground props to the generated Local shadow casters collection when needed.

Automatic clipping also reduces the camera's far clip to a local horizon range inside the atmosphere. A planetary far clip caused Eevee to lose nearby tree shadows even with the local caster patch. Interior sky is rendered as a World background, so it remains visible with the shorter clip. **Minimum view distance** sets a floor for this range (default about 6.4 km on Earth); the calculated horizon can extend it. Raise it for distant landmarks, checking Eevee's small shadows again. The near clip also adapts to keep the far/near ratio at or below one million; a larger ratio caused Eevee to lose sky-ray precision. Disable automatic clipping only when manually setting a suitable range for the shot; extremely distant objects can otherwise be clipped in a close view.

## Rendering and remaining limits

Close views now use native Principled materials and actual shadows. This makes scene lamps meaningful. Sun intensity/direction come from the generator's lights; soft fill provides a baseline for both engines. The sky uses a CC0 photographed HDRI for interior views and environmental reflections. Its brightness eases in below the atmosphere boundary; orbital cloud opacity decreases along with this transition and the shell is hidden below the cloud layer. The analytic atmospheric shell remains an approximation. Orbital clouds remain shells; interior views use the HDRI's photographed clouds. This is not a volumetric cloud fly-through simulation.

Both engines receive the same geometry and model LODs. Native PBR shadowing, indirect light, alpha sampling and reflections can differ, so the old sub-percent orbital comparison must not be applied to these new views. Current paired images and measured differences are under `output/planets/living/`. Render quality and photographic appearance still depend on terrain settings, camera placement, lighting and the selected assets. The finite grass/tree library does not generate every ecosystem or individual leaf as closed solid geometry. No foliage/rock collision avoidance, ocean breaking simulation, river simulation or motion-blur/subframe guarantee is included.

The current 960 × 540 paired renders have mean display-space RGB differences of **2.67% grassland, 4.86% forest, 1.42% ocean, 0.53% alpine and 1.44% desert**. These averages do not mean each pixel matches that closely; foliage and highlights have larger local differences. The generator is a working realism-oriented prototype. The small asset library, visible distant ground-cover simplification and approximate atmosphere still keep some views below the requested photographic standard.

## Rebuild and verification

`tools/fetch_biome_assets.py`, `fetch_ground_pbr.py` and `fetch_real_terrain.py` retrieve source data. `prepare_biome_assets.py` prepares the packed source LOD library. `build_living_planet.py` builds the shot and paired renders. `tests/blender_living.py` checks actual displacement, wave agreement, model/terrain caps, deterministic placement and existing-scene scaling. `living_verification.json` records the rendered scene's counts and timings. `living_metrics.json` measures display-space image differences; these are not physical radiance errors.
